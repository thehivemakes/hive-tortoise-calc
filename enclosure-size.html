How the 10x5 Tortoise Enclosure Rule Works

A frequently cited rule of thumb among tortoise keepers is that an indoor enclosure should provide a minimum floor area of about ten times the tortoise's shell length by five times its shell length. A tortoise with a 6-inch shell works out to roughly a 60-by-30-inch floor area — about 12.5 square feet — under this guideline.

This is a minimum, not a target. Tortoises are active, ground-covering animals, and most keepers who have the room provide more space than the rule requires. Multiple tortoises housed together generally need close to the full minimum floor area for each additional animal, not a fraction of it, since they still need to move, forage, and get out of each other's way.

Outdoor housing needs are larger because outdoor pens have to accommodate temperature swings, digging, grazing, and weather-driven basking behavior that a small indoor enclosure does not. A commonly used starting point is 3–4x the indoor minimum; this calculator uses roughly 4x by doubling both the length and width.
